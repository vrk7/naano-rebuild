Every one of those cuts is the same rule applied thirteen times. `PRODUCT.md` states one test:

> A brand can point at a single published post and see the people who engaged with it, the companies they work for, and which of its ICPs those companies match.

Cut is not a size or effort judgement — it's "does building this change whether that sentence is demonstrable?" For all thirteen the answer is no. But the *way* they fail the test differs, and that's the actual reasoning. They sort into six arguments:

## 1. Solved, conventional, proves nothing

**Earnings/payouts/Stripe/invoices · i18n · messaging · multi-workspace + team invites.**

These are real work, but they're work anyone can do and everyone has done. Shipping a Stripe payout flow demonstrates that you can build a Stripe payout flow. It says nothing about whether a post produced pipeline. Money movement is *orthogonal* to the claim — hence "the ledger records commitments; nothing leaves." The commitment is the only part the claim touches (a brand's budget being held against a booking); the transfer isn't.

Messaging is the same shape with an extra edge: naano gates a thread on a booking (`brand/26`, `creator/33`), so a chat inbox is a whole second product bolted to the side. The rebuild keeps the *information* a thread would carry — state, a note on every event, the request-changes note — and drops the inbox.

## 2. It's a second, competing attribution model

**Affiliate program, Deal Link, referral attribution, leaderboard.**

This is the strongest cut in the list and the bullet undersells it. The entire product is *one* attribution story: post → engagement → person → company → ICP. Deal Link introduces a completely different one: brand signup → attributed to a creator's card → 25% of commission for 3 months (`creator/28`–`creator/32`).

Two attribution models in one product means two different answers to "where did this come from," and a user cannot tell which number to trust. It doesn't just fail to help the claim — it dilutes it.

## 3. It structurally *cannot* produce the evidence

**Externally managed / agency campaigns.**

`creator/21` is explicit: "This collaboration is not contracted, managed or paid through Naano." Contracting, communication, and payment all happen off-platform with an agency in between. So no post gets recorded here, no engagement is sampled, no leads exist. Building the one flow that by definition generates zero attribution data, in a product whose whole thesis is attribution, is what "perverse" is doing in that sentence.

## 4. It multiplies the tested core without testing the thesis

**Negotiation/counter-offers · bundles.**

The state machine is the load-bearing tested object — 11 states, guards, ~1,080 lines of tests. Counter-offers add a negotiation loop (offered → countered → re-offered → …), and every new state multiplies the guard combinations that need testing. The payoff: the answer to "did this post produce pipeline" is identical whether the price was negotiated or listed.

There's a second reason on this one that matters for method: recon marks whether a creator can even counter as **`NOT SEEN`** (`brand/14`). Building it would mean inventing behavior never observed — which the recon discipline forbids.

Bundles are the pricing-surface version of the same argument: every price becomes "per post or per bundle," across the marketplace, the profile, booking, and the ledger.

## 5. It's actively broken in the original — fix the cause instead

**Guided tours, checklists, nudge toasts.**

This one isn't "unnecessary," it's "observed to be harmful." Recon caught three guided layers running simultaneously and colliding — on `brand/15` a tour popover covers the form it's describing *and* truncates its own text.

More importantly, tours are a symptom treatment. The disease is that every naano surface is empty on arrival (§6 of the notes: Collaborations, Leads, Messages, Invoices, all KPIs at 0). A tour exists to narrate an empty product. Seed the workspace with published posts and leads already on them, and there's nothing left for a tour to do.

## 6. It serves the wrong party, or produces an unusable number

**The creator's analytics dashboard** (`creator/13`, `creator/14`) serves the creator's interest — proving their own reach — not the brand's question. It also inherits the biggest fake in the build: it's fed by the same scraped import ("No personal LinkedIn connection is required") that SCOPE.md already established can't be obtained legitimately. Paying the largest honesty cost in the project to serve the party the claim isn't addressed to.

**The shareable card** — flip animation, preview, share sheet (`creator/07`–`creator/09`) — is craft with no argument attached.

**Model-judged draft checks** is the subtlest. An LLM scoring "brief adherence: 78" gives a creator a number they can neither dispute nor act on — there's no next move. A deterministic check says *"must_mention 'Atira' — not found"* and points at the span it judged. One is arguable and actionable; the other is a vibe with a decimal point. It's also the least differentiated thing available: anyone can call a model.

## The pattern underneath: cut the surface, keep the seam

Several cuts deliberately leave a hook so they return as an addition, not a rewrite:
